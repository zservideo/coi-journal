## Why tie payment to insurance compliance?

A deficiency notice asks a trade to call its broker. A held payment makes the trade call today. [EPIC's 2026 guidance on trade risk](https://www.epicbrokers.com/insights/aligning-subcontractor-risk/) recommends contract provisions that allow payment to be withheld when insurance coverage is incomplete or misaligned with the contract.

## What does the subcontract need to say?

The control is only as good as the subcontract language behind it. The pieces to cover with counsel:

- **The condition.** Current certificates and endorsements meeting the [project requirements](/reports/reading-a-coi-against-requirements/) are a condition of payment.
- **The scope.** Whether a gap holds the whole payment or only part of it.
- **The cure.** How a trade fixes it, and how fast payment follows.
- **The law.** State prompt payment statutes ([AGC keeps a state-by-state map](https://agc.org/prompt-payment-state-state-map), and [IRMI explains how they interact with pay-when-paid clauses](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/pay-when-paid-clauses-and-prompt-payment-acts)) and public contract rules may limit what you can hold and for how long. This is where counsel earns their fee.

## How does an insurance check fit the monthly pay application cycle?

1. **Before each pay application is approved,** the compliance desk confirms the trade's file is current: no expired policies, no open deficiencies past their due date.
2. **If it isn't,** the PM gets a short note: which trade, what's missing, since when.
3. **The trade gets notice** with the pay app, naming the specific item and the fix.
4. **Payment releases** when the item clears, on the next cycle or sooner if your process allows.

Table: The insurance check in a monthly pay application cycle
| Step | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the trade's file current before the pay app is approved? | No expired policies, no deficiencies past due |
| 2 | If not, who needs to know? | The PM gets the trade, the missing item and the date |
| 3 | What does the trade hear? | A notice with the pay app naming the item and the fix |
| 4 | When does payment move? | When the item clears, on the next cycle or sooner |

The point is to make compliance a routine step, not an escalation. When it's routine, it's not personal.

## How do you keep a payment hold fair?

- **Same rule for every trade.** Announce it at award, in writing.
- **Specific notices.** "Missing CG 20 37 for policy GL-123, expired 10/1" gets fixed. See [completed operations](/dictionary/completed-operations/) and our paper on [fixing gaps between brokers and GCs](/reports/fixing-gaps-brokers-gcs/). "Insurance noncompliant" gets an argument.
- **Fast release.** Hold no longer than the gap lasts.

## What other payment controls do GCs use?

[IRMI's guidance on struggling trades](https://irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/tackling-subcontractor-performance-issues-from-the-ground-up) lists other payment-side tools, including joint checks, payroll support, direct purchase of materials and more frequent pay applications. Those address financial risk. Insurance compliance is a separate condition and works best kept separate.

Pair this with a [defensible COI file](/reports/defensible-coi-file/), so every hold has a documented reason.
