## What is the certificate holder box?

The ACORD 25 has a certificate holder box in the lower left. It names the person or organization the certificate was issued to, usually the GC, the owner or both. The broker fills it in from the request.

## What does the ACORD 25 say about the certificate holder?

The top of the [ACORD 25](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/acord-25-2025-12-liability.pdf) carries a disclaimer. In substance: the certificate is issued as a matter of information only, confers no rights on the certificate holder, and doesn't amend, extend or alter the coverage afforded by the policies. It also says that if the certificate holder is an additional insured, the policies must have additional insured provisions or be endorsed.

That's the form telling you, in capital letters, that the holder box isn't coverage. See our white paper on why [a certificate is not coverage](/reports/certificate-is-not-coverage/).

## Is the certificate holder an additional insured?

Table: Certificate holder versus additional insured
| | Certificate holder | Additional insured |
|---|---|---|
| Where it lives | A box on the certificate | An endorsement to the policy |
| What it gives you | A copy of information | Rights under the trade's policy |
| Who decides | The broker, on request | The insurer, by endorsement |

A GC can be the holder without being an additional insured, and an additional insured without being the holder. Your requirements almost certainly ask for both. Only the endorsement, such as [CG 20 37 for completed operations](https://insurancexdate.com/insurance-forms/CG/CG-20-37/), proves the second. See [additional insured](/dictionary/additional-insured/).

## Why does the holder box still matter?

- **Delivery.** It's where the broker sends updated certificates.
- **Your file.** It shows the certificate was issued for your project, not borrowed from another job.
- **Cancellation.** The form's cancellation wording says notice will be delivered in accordance with policy provisions. Being the holder doesn't create a notice right. A notice endorsement does. See [notice of cancellation](/dictionary/notice-of-cancellation/).

## Which certificate holder naming mistakes matter?

1. **Wrong legal entity.** "Smith Builders" on the certificate, "Smith Builders of Massachusetts, LLC" on the subcontract. Match the legal name exactly.
2. **Missing owner or lender.** Your requirements may name parties beyond the GC. List them in the holder box or description, and make sure the endorsement schedule names them too.
3. **Project not identified.** A blank description makes it hard to show the certificate was issued for this job.
4. **Holder named, endorsement silent.** The most common version of this gap, and the one [IRMI's commentary on certificates that looked fine](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/the-insurance-certificate-looked-fine) is about. The fix is the endorsement, not a new certificate.
