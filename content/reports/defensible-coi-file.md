## What makes a COI file defensible?

Picture the conversation after a serious claim. Your carrier, the owner or opposing counsel asks: what did your requirements say, what did the trade send, what did you check, what was wrong, and what did you do about it? A defensible file answers all five without anyone's memory.

## What records belong in a defensible COI file?

1. **The requirement.** The project's requirements as they stood on the date of review, with the contract clause each line came from. See [per project requirements](/reports/per-project-requirements-and-renewals/).
2. **The evidence.** Every certificate and endorsement received, with the date received. Keep the old ones when new ones arrive.
3. **The review.** Line by line, what passed, what failed and why. A checklist with dates beats a green checkmark with none.
4. **The follow-up.** Each deficiency, who was asked to fix it, when, and the answer. See [fixing gaps between brokers and GCs](/reports/fixing-gaps-brokers-gcs/).
5. **The decisions.** Any exception: who approved letting the trade work with a gap open, and why. In writing.
6. **The diary.** Renewal requests and what came back.

Table: The five questions after a claim, and the record that answers each
| Question | Record that answers it |
|---|---|
| What did your requirements say? | The project's requirements on the review date, with the contract clause behind each line |
| What did the trade send? | Every certificate and endorsement, with the date received |
| What did you check? | A dated, line-by-line review |
| What was wrong? | The review's failed lines and why they failed |
| What did you do about it? | Each follow-up, the answer, and any written exception |

[IRMI's 2019 guidance](https://irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/tackling-subcontractor-performance-issues-from-the-ground-up) on handling trade performance problems puts it simply: keep full, contemporaneous documentation of events. Insurance review is one more event worth documenting as it happens.

## Why keep superseded certificates?

Claims are measured against what was in force on the date of loss. If the only certificate in your file is this year's renewal, you can't show what you had last spring. Keep every version, labeled with its dates.

## How long should a GC keep COI records?

[ABC Carolinas' 2026 guidance](https://abccarolinas.org/subcontractor-management-strategic-risk-and-competitive-advantage-for-carolinas-commercial-contractors-in-2026/) points to retention of three to seven years after completion. Completed operations claims can arrive later than that, so many GCs keep the file as long as the completed operations requirement in the subcontract runs, plus a margin. That's a call for your counsel. See [completed operations](/dictionary/completed-operations/).

## What do federal contracts require a GC to keep?

On covered federal contracts, [FAR 52.228-5](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.228-5) requires the prime to keep copies of subcontractors' proof of insurance and make them available to the contracting officer on request. See our brief on [federal jobs](/reports/federal-contracts-far-insurance/).

## What makes a COI file weak?

- Certificates with no record of what they were checked against.
- A deficiency email with no answer and no decision.
- Only the current certificate, with older ones overwritten.
- Endorsements stored apart from the certificates they belong to, so nobody can show they match.
