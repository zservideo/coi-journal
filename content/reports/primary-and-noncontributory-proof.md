## What the words mean

When two policies cover the same loss, each policy's other insurance condition decides who pays first and whether the payers share. Left alone, a trade contractor's general liability policy may treat the GC's own policy as co-primary, or even excess, for a claim where the GC is an additional insured. That's the opposite of what most subcontracts intend.

Two words fix it, if the policy actually contains them:

- **Primary.** The trade's policy responds first, before the GC's.
- **Noncontributory.** The trade's insurer won't seek contribution from the GC's insurer for that loss.

Together they keep a claim caused by the trade's work on the trade's policy, where the subcontract put it. The GC's loss history, deductible and limits stay out of it. See [primary and noncontributory](/dictionary/primary-and-noncontributory/) in the Dictionary.

## The standard endorsement

ISO introduced CG 20 01, Primary and Noncontributory, Other Insurance Condition, as an optional endorsement in its 2013 general liability changes. Claims Journal's review of those changes said it was introduced in response to wording found in many construction contracts. A later 12 19 edition is also in use.

The endorsement amends the policy's other insurance condition. Under it, the trade's insurance is primary to, and won't seek contribution from, other insurance available to an additional insured, when two conditions are met.

## The two conditions

1. **The additional insured is a named insured on the other policy.** The GC's own policy, where the GC is the named insured, qualifies. A policy where the GC is itself only an additional insured may not.
2. **A written contract requires it.** The trade must have agreed in writing that its insurance would be primary and noncontributory. No written requirement, no primary status.

The second condition is the one that bites. If the subcontract is unsigned, out of date, or silent on primary and noncontributory, the endorsement can be on the policy and still not apply.

## What CG 20 01 doesn't do

- **It doesn't create additional insured status.** The GC has to be an additional insured through a separate endorsement, such as CG 20 10 for ongoing operations and CG 20 37 for completed operations. See our white paper on [additional insured endorsements](/reports/additional-insured-endorsements/).
- **It doesn't reach the umbrella by itself.** Claims Journal noted it applies to the general liability policy, not an umbrella attached to it. If your requirements call for primary and noncontributory excess coverage, you need the excess policy's own wording.
- **It doesn't waive subrogation.** IRMI notes that a waiver of subrogation endorsement doesn't create primary and noncontributory coverage, and the reverse is true too. They're separate requirements. See our brief on [waivers of subrogation](/reports/waiver-of-subrogation-endorsements/).

## How to confirm it

A certificate can't confirm it. The ACORD 25 has no box that proves primary and noncontributory status, and a phrase in the description of operations is information only. To confirm:

1. **Ask for the endorsement itself.** CG 20 01 or the carrier's equivalent, attached to the policy listed on the certificate.
2. **Check the policy number and dates match** the general liability policy on the certificate.
3. **Read the conditions against your paper.** Is the subcontract signed, current and explicit about primary and noncontributory coverage? If not, fix the contract, not the certificate.
4. **Check the excess layer separately** if your requirements extend primary and noncontributory status to the umbrella.
5. **Diary the renewal.** Endorsements don't automatically carry over to the new policy.

## The short version

Primary and noncontributory is contract language that has to be met by policy language. The certificate tells you someone typed the words. The endorsement, read against a signed subcontract, tells you they apply.
