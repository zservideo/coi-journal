## Why GCs ask for so much

A GC is on the hook to its owner for every trade on the job. Its own insurer and the owner expect it to prove each trade carries what the contract requires. Munich Re's guidance to contractors, for example, recommends getting a certificate before engaging any new trade and rechecking it at least yearly. EPIC's guidance is blunter: a COI isn't enough. That's why the request list keeps growing. See [why contractors send certificates](/reports/why-contractors-send-certificates/).

## Before you sign

1. **Read the insurance exhibit.** Limits, endorsements, completed operations duration, carrier rating, deductible caps.
2. **Send it to your broker** and ask one question: can my current policies meet this, and if not, what does it cost?
3. **Raise gaps at bid.** "We carry $1 million per occurrence, not $2 million" is an easy conversation before award and a hard one after.

## What to send, the first time

- **A certificate for this project,** with the GC and any other required parties in the holder box and the project named in the description.
- **Additional insured endorsements,** ongoing and completed operations, with schedules or blanket wording that covers this GC.
- **Primary and noncontributory and waiver of subrogation endorsements,** on each policy the contract names.
- **Per project aggregate endorsement,** if required.
- **Workers comp proof,** or the state fund certificate in Ohio, North Dakota, Washington or Wyoming.

Send it as one packet. A GC reviewing the certificate, then waiting a week for endorsements, then a week for the umbrella, is how a three-day review becomes a month.

## What to ask your broker for

- **The endorsement forms on your policies,** by form number, so you know what you can and can't provide.
- **Blanket endorsements** where possible, so every new GC doesn't need a new filing.
- **A renewal packet** issued before your policies expire, not after.

## Common reasons for a hold

| Hold | Fix |
|---|---|
| Certificate only, no endorsements | Send the endorsements |
| GC's legal name doesn't match | Reissue with the exact name |
| Limit below requirement | Umbrella or a policy change |
| Expired policy | Send the renewal |
| Completed operations not covered | Ask your broker for completed operations AI wording |
| Exclusion conflicts with your scope | Talk to your broker before the job, not during |

## The habit that saves the most time

Keep a folder per GC with its current requirements and the last packet you sent. When a renewal comes, update the packet and send it to every GC you're working for in the same week.
