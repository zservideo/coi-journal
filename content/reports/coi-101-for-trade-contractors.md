## Why do GCs ask trade contractors for so many insurance documents?

A GC is on the hook to its owner for every trade on the job. Its own insurer and the owner expect it to prove each trade carries what the contract requires. [Munich Re's guidance to contractors](https://www.munichre.com/specialty/north-america/en/insights/construction-and-engineering/proactively-evaluating-subcontractors-a-key-to-mitigating-risk.html), for example, recommends getting a certificate before engaging any new trade and rechecking it at least yearly. [EPIC's guidance](https://www.epicbrokers.com/insights/aligning-subcontractor-risk/) is blunter: a COI isn't enough. That's why the request list keeps growing. See [why contractors send certificates](/reports/why-contractors-send-certificates/).

## What should a trade contractor check before signing?

1. **Read the insurance exhibit.** Limits, endorsements, completed operations duration, carrier rating, deductible caps.
2. **Send it to your broker** and ask one question: can my current policies meet this, and if not, what does it cost?
3. **Raise gaps at bid.** "We carry $1 million per occurrence, not $2 million" is an easy conversation before award and a hard one after.

## What should a trade contractor send with its certificate?

- **A certificate for this project** (usually the [ACORD 25](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/acord-25-2025-12-liability.pdf)), with the GC and any other required parties in the holder box and the project named in the description.
- **[Additional insured](/dictionary/additional-insured/) endorsements,** ongoing and completed operations, with schedules or blanket wording that covers this GC.
- **[Primary and noncontributory](/dictionary/primary-and-noncontributory/) and [waiver of subrogation](/dictionary/waiver-of-subrogation/) endorsements,** on each policy the contract names.
- **Per project aggregate endorsement,** if required.
- **Workers comp proof,** or the state fund certificate in Ohio, North Dakota, Washington or Wyoming. See [monopolistic states](/reports/monopolistic-workers-comp-states/).

Send it as one packet. A GC reviewing the certificate, then waiting a week for endorsements, then a week for the umbrella, is how a three-day review becomes a month.

## What should a trade contractor ask its broker for?

- **The endorsement forms on your policies,** by form number, so you know what you can and can't provide.
- **Blanket endorsements** where possible, so every new GC doesn't need a new filing.
- **A renewal packet** issued before your policies expire, not after.

## Why do GCs put trade certificates on hold?

Table: Common reasons a GC holds a trade's certificate, and the fix
| Hold | Fix |
|---|---|
| Certificate only, no endorsements | Send the endorsements |
| GC's legal name doesn't match | Reissue with the exact name |
| Limit below requirement | Umbrella or a policy change |
| Expired policy | Send the renewal |
| Completed operations not covered | Ask your broker for completed operations AI wording |
| Exclusion conflicts with your scope | Talk to your broker before the job, not during |

## What habit gets insurance approved fastest?

Keep a folder per GC with its current requirements and the last packet you sent. When a renewal comes, update the packet and send it to every GC you're working for in the same week.
