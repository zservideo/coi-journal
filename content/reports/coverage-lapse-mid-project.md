## The lapse you can't see

Coverage stops for ordinary reasons. A policy reaches its expiration date and isn't renewed. A carrier cancels for nonpayment. A renewal is written by a new carrier, with different endorsements. A contractor changes brokers and something falls through.

None of those events changes the certificate in your file. It still shows the old dates and the old boxes. And the form doesn't promise to tell you: the ACORD 25 cancellation box says notice "will be delivered in accordance with the policy provisions." Many policies only require notice to the named insured. See [notice of cancellation](/dictionary/notice-of-cancellation/).

## What's exposed

When a trade's coverage lapses, its losses don't disappear. They look for the next pocket.

- **Injuries to the trade's own workers.** If workers' comp lapses, an injured worker may look to the GC. General liability usually excludes the insured's own employees, so it won't fill the hole. In a 2026 Illinois ruling [reported by Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/insurer-prevails-in-coverage-dispute-over-carpenters-death/), a general liability carrier owed no indemnity for a carpenter's death because the employee injury exclusion applied and the contractor had no workers' comp in force.
- **Third-party injury and property damage.** Without the trade's general liability, and the additional insured endorsement on it, claims arising from the trade's work fall to the GC's own policy. That can mean a deductible, a claim on the GC's loss history and a harder renewal.
- **Completed operations.** A trade that drops its policy after finishing can leave you without the completed operations coverage your contract required. Defect claims often arrive later. See [completed operations](/dictionary/completed-operations/).
- **The owner relationship.** If the prime contract required the GC to make sure every trade stayed insured, a lapse is also the GC's contract problem.

## The playbook

**Before the job.** Diary every expiration date for every policy on every certificate. Require renewal certificates and endorsements before each date, in writing, in the subcontract.

**Thirty days out.** Ask the trade and its broker for the renewal. A renewal certificate with no endorsements is a request for the endorsements, not a pass.

**On the expiration date with nothing in hand.** Treat the coverage as gone. Escalate to the trade's owner. Check your subcontract for the right to stop the trade's work, withhold payment, or buy coverage and back-charge.

**If you learn of a cancellation.** Confirm it with the broker. Stop the trade's work on site until coverage is replaced. Document every step and date.

**At close-out.** Before final payment and release of retainage, confirm completed operations coverage is in place for the period the contract requires, and keep diarying it for that period.

## The rule that saves the most

The simplest control is a sentence in every subcontract and a habit on every job: **no coverage, no work.** It turns a lapse from a hidden exposure into a work stoppage someone has to fix the same day.
