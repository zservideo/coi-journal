## Why do project requirements set a carrier rating floor?

A certificate shows a policy exists. It doesn't show whether the insurer behind it can pay a large claim years from now. Rating requirements, which we saw across the [public owner requirements we compared](/reports/public-owner-requirements-compared/), set a minimum financial strength for the carriers a GC will accept, for its own program and for every trade below it.

## What does A- VII mean?

Two parts, two different questions:

- **The letter grade** is AM Best's Financial Strength Rating. Per [Total CSR's glossary](https://totalcsr.com/insurance-glossary/a-m-best-company-rating-insurance/), the scale runs from A++ (Superior) down to D, with F for insurers in liquidation, and A++ through A- are commonly called secure.
- **The Roman numeral** is the Financial Size Category, from I to XV, based on policyholder surplus. It measures how big a cushion the insurer has, not how strong it is.

So A- VII means a strength rating of A- or better and a size class of VII or larger. A carrier rated A+ but in a small size class can fail the requirement.

Table: The two parts of an A- VII requirement
| Part | What it is | What A- VII asks for |
|---|---|---|
| Letter grade | AM Best Financial Strength Rating, A++ down to D, F in liquidation | A- or better |
| Roman numeral | Financial Size Category, I to XV, based on policyholder surplus | VII or larger |

## Does the certificate show the carrier's rating?

The ACORD 25 lists each insurer's name and NAIC number. It doesn't show the rating or size class. Some brokers add ratings in the description of operations. That's useful, but it's the broker's statement, and it can be out of date.

## How does a GC check a carrier's rating?

1. **Look up each insurer**, not just the first one on the certificate. General liability, auto, workers comp and umbrella can be written by different carriers.
2. **Match the legal entity.** Insurance groups have many companies with similar names. Use the NAIC number on the certificate to find the right one in [NAIC's company search](https://content.naic.org/index.php/cis_consumer_information.htm), then look up that company's rating. [Bankrate's explainer on AM Best](https://www.bankrate.com/insurance/am-best/) covers how the scale reads.
3. **Check both parts** of the requirement: grade and size class.
4. **Record what you saw and when.** Ratings move. A rating at award isn't a rating at renewal.
5. **Decide in advance what happens on a downgrade.** Some requirements say a trade must replace a carrier that falls below the floor. If yours don't, a downgrade mid-project leaves you arguing.

## What about state funds, self-insurers and surplus lines carriers?

- **State funds and self-insurers** usually aren't rated the same way. Write requirements that say what's acceptable instead. See our brief on [monopolistic states](/reports/monopolistic-workers-comp-states/).
- **Surplus lines carriers** are rated too, and there's no guaranty fund behind them, so the rating carries more weight. See [surplus lines carriers on certificates](/reports/surplus-lines-carriers-on-certificates/).
