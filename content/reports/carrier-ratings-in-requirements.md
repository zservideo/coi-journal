## Why owners set a floor

A certificate shows a policy exists. It doesn't show whether the insurer behind it can pay a large claim years from now. Rating requirements set a minimum financial strength for the carriers a GC will accept, for its own program and for every trade below it.

## Reading A- VII

Two parts, two different questions:

- **The letter grade** is AM Best's Financial Strength Rating. Per Total CSR's glossary, the scale runs from A++ (Superior) down to D, with F for insurers in liquidation, and A++ through A- are commonly called secure.
- **The Roman numeral** is the Financial Size Category, from I to XV, based on policyholder surplus. It measures how big a cushion the insurer has, not how strong it is.

So A- VII means a strength rating of A- or better and a size class of VII or larger. A carrier rated A+ but in a small size class can fail the requirement.

## What the certificate shows

The ACORD 25 lists each insurer's name and NAIC number. It doesn't show the rating or size class. Some brokers add ratings in the description of operations. That's useful, but it's the broker's statement, and it can be out of date.

## How to check

1. **Look up each insurer**, not just the first one on the certificate. General liability, auto, workers comp and umbrella can be written by different carriers.
2. **Match the legal entity.** Insurance groups have many companies with similar names. Use the NAIC number on the certificate to find the right one.
3. **Check both parts** of the requirement: grade and size class.
4. **Record what you saw and when.** Ratings move. A rating at award isn't a rating at renewal.
5. **Decide in advance what happens on a downgrade.** Some requirements say a trade must replace a carrier that falls below the floor. If yours don't, a downgrade mid-project leaves you arguing.

## Edge cases

- **State funds and self-insurers** usually aren't rated the same way. Write requirements that say what's acceptable instead. See our brief on [monopolistic states](/reports/monopolistic-workers-comp-states/).
- **Surplus lines carriers** are rated too, and there's no guaranty fund behind them, so the rating carries more weight. See [surplus lines carriers on certificates](/reports/surplus-lines-carriers-on-certificates/).
