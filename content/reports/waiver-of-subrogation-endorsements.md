## What subrogation is

When an insurer pays a claim, it usually takes over its insured's right to recover from whoever caused the loss. That's subrogation. If a trade's employee is hurt on your site and the trade's workers comp carrier pays, that carrier may sue the GC to get its money back, arguing the GC's site conditions caused the injury.

A waiver of subrogation is the trade's insurer agreeing, in advance, not to do that against parties named in the endorsement. Most subcontracts require one on general liability, auto and workers comp. See [waiver of subrogation](/dictionary/waiver-of-subrogation/) in the Dictionary.

## The general liability endorsement

The standard general liability policy doesn't use the word subrogation. The operative wording is a condition called Transfer of Rights of Recovery Against Others to Us. IRMI explains that the insured must transfer its recovery rights for paid losses and do nothing after a loss to impair them.

ISO's CG 24 04, Waiver of Transfer of Rights of Recovery Against Others to Us, amends that condition. The insurer waives recovery against the persons or organizations in its schedule, but only to the extent the insured had waived its own right of recovery against them, in writing, before the loss. Editions in use include 05 09 and 12 19. For blanket waivers, ISO also publishes CG 24 53.

## The workers comp endorsement

NCCI's WC 00 03 13, Waiver of Our Right to Recover From Others Endorsement, starts from the insurer's right to recover its payments from anyone liable for a covered injury. It then says the insurer won't enforce that right against the person or organization named in the schedule.

Two limits are written into the form:

- It applies only to the extent the insured performs work under a written contract that requires the waiver.
- It doesn't benefit anyone not named in the schedule.

Some state rating bureaus publish their own versions. Monopolistic state funds have their own rules.

## What a waiver doesn't do

- **It isn't additional insured status.** A waiver stops the trade's insurer from suing you. It doesn't make that insurer defend you. See our white paper on [additional insured endorsements](/reports/additional-insured-endorsements/).
- **It isn't primary and noncontributory.** IRMI notes the CG 24 04 waiver doesn't result in primary and noncontributory coverage. See our brief on [primary and noncontributory](/reports/primary-and-noncontributory-proof/).
- **It waives the insurer's rights, not the trade's.** IRMI's example: under CG 24 04 only the insurer has waived recovery. The trade itself can still pursue the GC directly, for example to recover its deductible, unless the subcontract waives that too.
- **It may be needed even where you're an additional insured.** IRMI walks through a case where a GC was an additional insured only for ongoing operations, so a completed operations claim against it wasn't protected by the rule that stops an insurer suing its own insured. A waiver would have closed that gap.

## How to confirm it

1. **Ask for the endorsement on each policy the contract names.** General liability, auto, workers comp and umbrella each need their own wording.
2. **Check the schedule.** Your entity is named, or the blanket wording covers parties the insured agreed in a written contract to waive.
3. **Check the contract.** Both forms depend on a written agreement made before the loss. A signed, current subcontract is part of the proof.
4. **Diary renewals.** A waiver on last year's policy doesn't carry over.

A checked box on the ACORD 25 says a waiver exists. It doesn't show the schedule, the policy it's on, or the conditions. The endorsement does.
