## When the clause applies

FAR 28.310 tells contracting officers to include 52.228-5 when a fixed-price contract is expected to exceed the simplified acquisition threshold and requires work on a government installation. There are exceptions, such as contracts with only a small amount of work on the installation. Contracting officers can include it anyway when it's in the government's interest.

## The minimums

FAR 28.307-2 sets the floor. The contract schedule can raise it, and agency supplements often do.

| Coverage | FAR 28.307-2 minimum |
|---|---|
| Workers comp | As required by applicable federal and state statutes |
| Employer's liability | $100,000, except in states with exclusive or monopolistic funds |
| General liability | Bodily injury of at least $500,000 per occurrence; property damage only in special circumstances set by the agency |
| Auto liability | $200,000 per person and $500,000 per occurrence bodily injury; $20,000 per occurrence property damage |

These numbers are low next to most private project requirements. The other rules are where federal jobs differ.

## The notice endorsement

Under 52.228-5(b), before work starts the contractor must notify the contracting officer in writing that the required insurance is in place. The policies must carry an endorsement that any cancellation or material change adversely affecting the government's interest isn't effective until the longer of the period state law sets or 30 days after written notice to the contracting officer.

That's a policy requirement, not a certificate requirement. The ACORD 25's cancellation box is information only. See [notice of cancellation](/dictionary/notice-of-cancellation/) and our brief on [ACORD 25 wording](/reports/acord-25-limits-wording/).

## The flow-down

Paragraph (c) requires the prime to insert the substance of the clause, including the flow-down paragraph itself, in subcontracts that require work on a government installation. Subcontractors must provide and maintain the required insurance. The prime must keep copies of subcontractors' proofs of insurance and make them available to the contracting officer on request.

So the prime's file is part of compliance. A missing trade certificate is a contract problem, not just a risk problem.

## A federal job checklist

1. **Read the schedule** for limits above the FAR minimums and any agency clauses.
2. **Flow 52.228-5 down** in every subcontract with installation work.
3. **Collect the notice endorsement** from each trade, on each required policy.
4. **Keep the file ready** for the contracting officer: certificate, endorsements, policy dates, per trade.
5. **Diary renewals.** The requirement runs for the whole period of performance.

For how to organize that file, see our paper on [per project requirements and renewals](/reports/per-project-requirements-and-renewals/).
