## Why does a trade's file need to be complete on day one?

Once a trade is mobilized, holding it off site costs schedule. That pressure is why so many files stay incomplete. [EPIC's 2026 guidance](https://www.epicbrokers.com/insights/aligning-subcontractor-risk/) makes a related point: small purchase orders issued without a signed subcontract can leave the prime fully exposed, so tie them to a master agreement.

## What documents should a GC collect before a trade starts?

**1. A signed subcontract or master agreement.** Not a draft, not a PO alone. [Sentry's guidance](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/construction-safety/safety-equipment-other/article/22929257/sentry-insurance-6-effective-controls-for-mitigating-subcontractor-risk) is blunt: a handshake isn't enough, and a written agreement belongs on even small jobs. It also matters for the insurance: CG 20 01's primary and noncontributory condition, the CG 24 04 waiver and NCCI's WC 00 03 13 waiver each depend on a written contract that required them. See our briefs on [primary and noncontributory](/reports/primary-and-noncontributory-proof/) and [waivers of subrogation](/reports/waiver-of-subrogation-endorsements/).

**2. A certificate for this project.** [Munich Re recommends](https://www.munichre.com/specialty/north-america/en/insights/construction-and-engineering/proactively-evaluating-subcontractors-a-key-to-mitigating-risk.html) getting a certificate before engaging any new trade, one that meets the minimums of the contractor, the owner and regulators. Check it against your [project requirements](/reports/reading-a-coi-against-requirements/), not a generic list.

**3. The endorsements.** Additional insured (ongoing and completed operations), primary and noncontributory, waivers, per project aggregate and notice, each matched to the policy number on the certificate. EPIC's phrase for why: a COI is not enough.

**4. Workers comp proof.** A policy on the certificate, a state fund certificate in [monopolistic states](/reports/monopolistic-workers-comp-states/), or a documented exemption. Verify in a public lookup where one exists. See our brief on [workers comp lookups](/reports/workers-comp-coverage-lookups/).

**5. License and registration.** Sentry includes proof of licensing in its pre-signing checks.

**6. A named owner for open items.** If anything is missing, a person and a date, not a note in the file.

**7. The renewal diary.** Every policy expiration date, set now.

## Who owns each piece of the onboarding file?

Table: Who usually owns each piece of a new trade's file
| Piece | Usually owned by |
|---|---|
| Signed subcontract | Project manager or contracts |
| Certificate and endorsements | Compliance desk or project admin |
| Exclusions review on risky scopes | Risk manager or broker |
| Site orientation | Superintendent |
| Renewal diary | Compliance desk |

Sentry and EPIC both recommend a site walk-through or pre-start meeting to set expectations. It's also a good moment to confirm that the crew on site belongs to the entity on the certificate.

## What rule keeps onboarding files complete?

No complete file, no mobilization, unless someone with authority signs off on the exception in writing. That rule is easier to hold when the file is short and everyone knows what's on it.
