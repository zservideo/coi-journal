## What does surplus lines mean on a certificate?

Admitted insurers are licensed in a state and file their rates and forms there. Surplus lines insurers aren't. [NAIC describes the surplus lines market](https://content.naic.org/insurance-topics/surplus-lines) as non-admitted, specialized insurers covering risks not available in the admitted market. Hard-to-place construction risks, including high-hazard trades, New York work and large excess layers, often land there.

NAIC says the market grew 12.2% in 2024 to $131 billion, about 12% of a property and casualty market of more than $1 trillion. By premium, U.S. insurers wrote 75%, Lloyd's 16% and other non-U.S. insurers 9%.

## How are surplus lines insurers regulated?

- **U.S.-domiciled surplus lines insurers** are overseen by their home state.
- **Non-U.S. insurers and Lloyd's syndicates** appear on the NAIC Quarterly Listing of Alien Insurers and are overseen through NAIC's International Insurers Department.
- **The transaction** goes through a licensed surplus lines broker, who pays the premium tax to the insured's home state.

Table: Admitted and surplus lines insurers compared
| | Admitted insurer | Surplus lines insurer |
|---|---|---|
| Licensed in the insured's state | Yes | No, eligible instead |
| Rates and forms | Filed with the state | Not filed; forms vary more |
| Placed through | Any licensed agent or broker | A licensed surplus lines broker |
| State guaranty fund | Covers claims up to statutory limits | Not available, per NAIC |

## Is there a guaranty fund behind a surplus lines policy?

If an admitted insurer becomes insolvent, state guaranty funds pay covered claims up to statutory limits. NAIC states plainly that guaranty fund coverage isn't available for surplus lines policies. NAIC also notes surplus lines insolvency rates have historically been low. Low isn't zero, and on a construction defect claim that arrives years later, it's the insurer's balance sheet that pays.

## What should a GC check on a surplus lines carrier?

1. **Read your requirements.** Do they accept non-admitted carriers? Many do, with a rating floor. If yours are silent, decide.
2. **Check the carrier's financial strength rating** against your floor. [AM Best](https://www.bankrate.com/insurance/am-best/) is the rating most requirements name. See our brief on [carrier ratings](/reports/carrier-ratings-in-requirements/).
3. **Check eligibility.** For a non-U.S. insurer, look for it on the NAIC Quarterly Listing. For a U.S. surplus lines insurer, the state where the trade is based lists eligible carriers. The NAIC number on the [certificate](/dictionary/certificate-of-insurance/) lets you find the exact company in [NAIC's company search](https://content.naic.org/index.php/cis_consumer_information.htm).
4. **Watch the forms.** Surplus lines policies aren't bound to ISO forms. Exclusions and [additional insured](/dictionary/additional-insured/) wording vary more, so the endorsement review matters more. See our white paper on [exclusions behind the certificate](/reports/rented-falsified-excluded/).
5. **Diary renewals.** Surplus lines placements can move between carriers year to year.
