## Why does California's anti-indemnity law matter for certificates?

GCs ask for [additional insured](/dictionary/additional-insured/) status partly to back up the subcontract's indemnity. In California, the indemnity a contract can require is limited by statute. The insurance requirement is limited along with it.

## What does California Civil Code 2782 limit?

Per [Construction Defect Journal's analysis](https://www.constructiondefectjournal.com/archives/inside-issue/2012/01/ever-growing-thicket-california-civil-code-section-2782), a 2011 amendment, effective January 1, 2012, reshaped section 2782, with substantive changes for contracts entered on or after January 1, 2013:

- **Public agencies.** Subdivision (b) makes void and unenforceable any provision that imposes liability for the public agency's active negligence on a contractor.
- **Private owners.** New subdivision (c) applies similar limits to contracts with private property owners who aren't acting as contractors or suppliers. A homeowner improving their own single family home is excluded.
- **Residential.** The residential rules from the 2008 amendment carry forward.

## What does Civil Code 2782.05 mean for GCs and trades?

The same amendment added [section 2782.05](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_civ_code_section_2782.05). Per the article, it voids provisions that require a trade to indemnify or insure a contractor for claims arising from that contractor's active negligence or willful misconduct, or to the extent claims fall outside the trade's scope of work. The section comes with a long list of exclusions, which is a reason to read it with counsel rather than summarize it in a subcontract.

## What does Insurance Code 11580.04 do to additional insured endorsements?

Insurance Code 11580.04 addresses additional insured endorsements directly. [Its text](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_ins_code_section_11580.04) says an additional insured endorsement issued by an admitted or nonadmitted insurer for the benefit of a public agency shall not provide any duty of indemnity coverage for the active negligence of the additional insured, where the indemnity would be invalid under subdivision (b) of section 2782. It also says any contract requirement to procure insurance that's invalid under the section is itself invalid.

Table: Where California limits indemnity and additional insured requirements
| Provision | Applies to | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Civil Code 2782(b) | Contracts with public agencies | Voids provisions that put liability for the agency's active negligence on a contractor |
| Civil Code 2782(c) | Contracts with private owners not acting as contractors or suppliers | Similar limits; a homeowner improving their own single family home is excluded |
| Civil Code 2782.05 | Contracts between contractors and trades | Voids requirements to indemnify or insure a contractor for its active negligence or willful misconduct, or beyond the trade's scope, with many exclusions |
| Insurance Code 11580.04 | Additional insured endorsements for public agencies | No duty of indemnity coverage for the additional insured's active negligence where 2782(b) voids the indemnity |

## What should a GC do on California projects?

- **Don't expect AI coverage to fill the gap the statute creates.** Where the law limits indemnity, the matching insurance is limited too.
- **Keep California requirements current.** Requirements written before 2013 may ask for indemnity and insurance the statute now voids.
- **Still collect the endorsements.** Additional insured coverage for the trade's own negligence, [completed operations](/dictionary/completed-operations/), primary and noncontributory wording and waivers all still matter. See our white paper on [additional insured endorsements](/reports/additional-insured-endorsements/).
- **Get counsel to write the clause.** The exclusions and case law are where the answers live. Texas limits the same requirements through its own statute; see our brief on [the Texas anti-indemnity act](/reports/texas-anti-indemnity-additional-insured/).
