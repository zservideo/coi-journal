## Why this matters for certificates

GCs ask for additional insured status partly to back up the subcontract's indemnity. In California, the indemnity a contract can require is limited by statute. The insurance requirement is limited along with it.

## Civil Code 2782

Per Construction Defect Journal's analysis, a 2011 amendment, effective January 1, 2012, reshaped section 2782, with substantive changes for contracts entered on or after January 1, 2013:

- **Public agencies.** Subdivision (b) makes void and unenforceable any provision that imposes liability for the public agency's active negligence on a contractor.
- **Private owners.** New subdivision (c) applies similar limits to contracts with private property owners who aren't acting as contractors or suppliers. A homeowner improving their own single family home is excluded.
- **Residential.** The residential rules from the 2008 amendment carry forward.

## Section 2782.05: GCs and trades

The same amendment added section 2782.05. Per the article, it voids provisions that require a trade to indemnify or insure a contractor for claims arising from that contractor's active negligence or willful misconduct, or to the extent claims fall outside the trade's scope of work. The section comes with a long list of exclusions, which is a reason to read it with counsel rather than summarize it in a subcontract.

## Insurance Code 11580.04: the insurance side

Insurance Code 11580.04 addresses additional insured endorsements directly. Its text says an additional insured endorsement issued by an admitted or nonadmitted insurer for the benefit of a public agency shall not provide any duty of indemnity coverage for the active negligence of the additional insured, where the indemnity would be invalid under subdivision (b) of section 2782. It also says any contract requirement to procure insurance that's invalid under the section is itself invalid.

## What to do with this

- **Don't expect AI coverage to fill the gap the statute creates.** Where the law limits indemnity, the matching insurance is limited too.
- **Keep California requirements current.** Requirements written before 2013 may ask for indemnity and insurance the statute now voids.
- **Still collect the endorsements.** Additional insured coverage for the trade's own negligence, completed operations, primary and noncontributory wording and waivers all still matter. See our white paper on [additional insured endorsements](/reports/additional-insured-endorsements/).
- **Get counsel to write the clause.** The exclusions and case law are where the answers live.
