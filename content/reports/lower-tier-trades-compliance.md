## Why do lower-tier trades create insurance risk?

A GC hires an electrical trade. The electrical trade hires a low-voltage crew. The low-voltage crew's employee is hurt. If the crew has no workers comp, or its liability policy excludes the work, the loss climbs the chain to the electrical trade, then to the GC.

## How should insurance requirements flow down to lower tiers?

[Munich Re's 2025 guidance](https://www.munichre.com/specialty/north-america/en/insights/construction-and-engineering/proactively-evaluating-subcontractors-a-key-to-mitigating-risk.html) recommends requiring trades' own subcontractors to meet the same standards. [ABC Carolinas' 2026 guidance](https://abccarolinas.org/subcontractor-management-strategic-risk-and-competitive-advantage-for-carolinas-commercial-contractors-in-2026/) makes the same point for labor compliance, calling for obligations to flow down to lower-tier trades. On federal jobs, [FAR 52.228-5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/48/52.228-5) requires the clause to flow into subcontracts that involve work on the installation. See [federal jobs](/reports/federal-contracts-far-insurance/).

What the subcontract should say:

1. **Same requirements.** Lower tiers carry the coverage your requirements set for their trade tier, or a defined subset.
2. **Same parties.** The GC and owner are [additional insureds](/dictionary/additional-insured/) on the lower tier's policies, not just on the first tier's.
3. **Same waivers.** [Waivers of subrogation](/dictionary/waiver-of-subrogation/) running to the GC and owner.
4. **Collection duty.** The first-tier trade collects and keeps the lower tier's certificate and endorsements.
5. **Access.** The GC can see them on request, and before the lower tier starts.

Table: Who does what below the first tier
| Party | Duty |
|---|---|
| GC | Writes the flow-down clause, keeps the list of lower tiers, asks to see certificates before a lower tier starts |
| First-tier trade | Puts the same requirements in its own subcontracts, collects and keeps each lower tier's certificate and endorsements |
| Lower-tier trade | Carries the coverage, additional insureds and waivers set for its trade tier |

## What should a GC collect for lower-tier trades?

At minimum, a list of every lower-tier trade on the job and a certificate for each. For higher-risk scopes, the endorsements too. Your file should show you asked, even if the first tier holds the documents.

## Where do lower-tier insurance gaps open?

- **Sole proprietors and 1099 crews.** Some states exempt sole proprietors from workers comp. ABC's 2026 guidance suggests a pre-award review of any trade using labor brokers or 1099-heavy models.
- **Labor brokers.** Whose workers comp covers the crew, and does it name your site?
- **Late additions.** Lower tiers hired mid-job, after the first tier's file was complete.
- **Certificates borrowed from the first tier.** A lower tier working under the first tier's certificate isn't covered by it.

## How can pay applications catch new lower-tier trades?

Make the lower-tier list part of each pay application. A new name on the list triggers a certificate request before payment for that work. See [tying pay applications to insurance compliance](/reports/pay-applications-and-insurance-compliance/).
