## Why is insurance at closeout different?

During the job, the risk is someone getting hurt or something getting damaged while work is under way. After it, the risk is a defect or a failure in finished work. Those are different coverages, and often different endorsements.

An additional insured endorsement for ongoing operations, such as CG 20 10, generally covers the GC for liability arising out of the trade's work while it's being performed. Completed operations needs its own wording, such as [CG 20 37](https://insurancexdate.com/insurance-forms/CG/CG-20-37/). [IRMI's 2013 commentary](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/subrogation-and-the-cgl-policy) works through an example of a GC that was an additional insured only for ongoing operations, which left it exposed on a completed operations claim. See [completed operations](/dictionary/completed-operations/) and our white paper on [additional insured endorsements](/reports/additional-insured-endorsements/).

Table: Ongoing and completed operations additional insured coverage
| | Ongoing operations | Completed operations |
|---|---|---|
| Example ISO form | CG 20 10 | CG 20 37 |
| Covers the GC for | Liability arising out of the trade's work while it's being performed | Liability arising out of the trade's finished work |
| When claims arrive | During the job | After the crews leave, sometimes years later |
| How long to require it | For the duration of the work | A set period after completion, often three to five years |

## How long does completed operations coverage need to last?

Requirements usually set a duration after completion. [ABC Carolinas' 2026 guidance](https://abccarolinas.org/subcontractor-management-strategic-risk-and-competitive-advantage-for-carolinas-commercial-contractors-in-2026/) cites three to five years of completed operations coverage. Public owners often tie the period to project size, as [Sonoma County's template](https://sonomacounty.gov/administrative-support-and-fiscal-services/human-resources/divisions-and-units/liability-and-insurance/insurance/insurance-requirements-for-contracts-and-agreements/view-all-insurance-requirement-templates/template-1) does. Check your prime contract: the GC's obligation to the owner sets the floor for what it needs from trades.

## What should a GC collect before final payment?

1. **A current certificate** showing products and completed operations limits.
2. **The completed operations additional insured endorsement**, matched to the current policy number.
3. **Confirmation the coverage will be maintained** for the required period, as the subcontract requires.
4. **Any per project or dedicated completed operations aggregate** the requirements call for. Remember CG 25 03 doesn't separate the completed operations aggregate. See [per project aggregate limits](/reports/per-project-aggregate-limits/).
5. **The final file review** signed off and dated. See [what a defensible COI file looks like](/reports/defensible-coi-file/).

## What happens after closeout?

The diary doesn't end at final payment. Each year during the completed operations period, request the trade's renewal certificate and endorsement. Trades change carriers, change forms and sometimes go out of business. A renewal request that bounces is information too.

## Why is final payment the moment to collect?

Before final payment, the GC holds retainage and the last check. After it, the GC holds a contract clause. Use closeout to get the paper while it's still easy to get.
