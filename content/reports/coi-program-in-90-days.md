## What should a GC do in the first 30 days?

**Inventory.** List every active project and every trade on each. Note where the current certificates live, if anywhere.

**Requirements.** For each project, write the insurance requirements as data: lines, limits, endorsements, parties, durations, carrier floor, each with its source clause. See our [data model for project requirements](/reports/project-requirements-data-model/).

**Standard subcontract.** Have counsel review the insurance article in your template. [EPIC's 2026 guidance](https://www.epicbrokers.com/insights/aligning-subcontractor-risk/) recommends an annual contract language review and a standard template.

**Owner.** Name one person who owns the program. A shared inbox isn't an owner.

## What happens in days 31 to 60?

**Intake.** One address for certificates, one packet request, one checklist. Use the request in our [COI 101 for trade contractors](/reports/coi-101-for-trade-contractors/) so trades know what to send.

**Review.** Every certificate against its project's requirements, line by line, with endorsements matched to policies. See [reading a COI against requirements](/reports/reading-a-coi-against-requirements/).

**Log.** Every gap, by trade and project, with the date found. This becomes your baseline.

**Lower tiers.** [ABC Carolinas' 2026 guidance](https://abccarolinas.org/subcontractor-management-strategic-risk-and-competitive-advantage-for-carolinas-commercial-contractors-in-2026/) calls for flowing compliance obligations down to lower-tier trades. Ask each trade for its list. See [lower-tier trades](/reports/lower-tier-trades-compliance/).

## How does a GC close the gaps in days 61 to 90?

**Follow-up.** Specific deficiency notices to the trade and its broker, with due dates. See [fixing gaps between brokers and GCs](/reports/fixing-gaps-brokers-gcs/), which also covers what the broker can and can't change.

**Renewal diary.** Every policy expiration, requested 30 days ahead. [Munich Re's guidance](https://www.munichre.com/specialty/north-america/en/insights/construction-and-engineering/proactively-evaluating-subcontractors-a-key-to-mitigating-risk.html) recommends rechecking proof of insurance at least yearly or before critical milestones.

**Payment link.** Make current compliance a condition of each pay application, as the subcontract allows. See [tying pay applications to insurance compliance](/reports/pay-applications-and-insurance-compliance/).

**New-trade rule.** No complete file, no mobilization. See [onboarding a new trade](/reports/onboarding-a-new-trade/).

**Report.** A one-page monthly summary for leadership.

Table: The 90-day plan at a glance
| Days | Focus | What exists at the end |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 30 | Write it down | An inventory, requirements as data with sources, a reviewed subcontract template and a named owner |
| 31 to 60 | One door | One intake address and checklist, every certificate reviewed against requirements, a gap log |
| 61 to 90 | Close and keep closed | Deficiency notices sent, a renewal diary, compliance tied to pay applications, a monthly report |

## What should a COI program measure?

- Trades with a complete file, by project.
- Open gaps, by type and age.
- Days from request to complete file.
- Renewals received before expiration.

Treat your first month's numbers as your baseline and measure against yourself. Industry gap rates are hard to verify, and we don't publish one until our own sample clears our [methodology](/methodology/).

## Where do COI programs break down?

- Requirements that live in one person's head.
- Endorsements filed apart from certificates.
- Renewals tracked in someone's calendar.
- Exceptions granted by phone with nothing written down.
