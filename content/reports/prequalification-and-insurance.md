## What is trade prequalification for?

Prequalification answers a narrow question: should this trade be allowed to bid? Turner & Townsend [frames it](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/business-services/coaching-consulting/article/22962587/turner-townsend-what-contractors-should-evaluate-before-awarding-subcontracts) as establishing baseline capability, with project-specific risk evaluated later at buyout. [Construction Dive's 2019 survey of GCs](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/the-dotted-line-how-contractors-are-finding-and-vetting-subcontractors/569056/) described Mortenson sending a qualification package with each request for proposal and independently verifying what came back.

Insurance belongs in that baseline, because a trade that can't buy the coverage your projects require will become a problem after award.

## What insurance checks belong before award?

The prequalification questions that matter for insurance:

- **Program shape.** Carriers, limits, deductibles and retentions by line. [ABC Carolinas' 2026 guidance](https://abccarolinas.org/subcontractor-management-strategic-risk-and-competitive-advantage-for-carolinas-commercial-contractors-in-2026/) lists typical ranges for general liability, auto, umbrella and workers comp, plus [additional insured](/dictionary/additional-insured/) status with primary and noncontributory wording and [completed operations](/dictionary/completed-operations/) coverage for three to five years.
- **Willingness.** [EPIC](https://www.epicbrokers.com/insights/aligning-subcontractor-risk/) lists the trade's willingness to meet insurance requirements as an assessment factor, and reluctance to share safety metrics or claims history as a red flag.
- **Consistency.** EPIC also flags inconsistent or outdated certificates. A trade whose certificates don't match each other is telling you something about its broker relationship.
- **Exclusions against scope.** EPIC calls out coverage exclusions that conflict with the trade's scope, such as height or trade-specific exclusions. Ask for the exclusions schedule now, not after award.
- **Safety record.** [Sentry's guidance](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/construction-safety/safety-equipment-other/article/22929257/sentry-insurance-6-effective-controls-for-mitigating-subcontractor-risk) includes OSHA violations, injury rates, recent losses and experience modification rate. These drive what the trade pays for insurance and whether it can get it.

## What insurance proof belongs after award?

Some proof can't exist until there's a contract. Additional insured endorsements that name your entities, per project aggregate schedules and waiver schedules all depend on the subcontract. Our paper on [reading a COI against requirements](/reports/reading-a-coi-against-requirements/) covers that review.

## How often should a GC re-qualify a trade?

[Munich Re's 2025 guidance](https://www.munichre.com/specialty/north-america/en/insights/construction-and-engineering/proactively-evaluating-subcontractors-a-key-to-mitigating-risk.html) recommends a formal review at engagement and regularly after, with a yearly review as a common practice and more frequent reviews for high-risk phases or new trades. It also recommends rechecking proof of insurance at least yearly or before critical milestones, because policies lapse, cancel or change.

## What insurance red flags should prompt a call?

- Certificates from the same trade that don't agree with each other.
- A broker who won't send endorsements.
- Height, residential or classification exclusions on a trade whose scope runs into them.
- A carrier below your rating floor. See [carrier ratings](/reports/carrier-ratings-in-requirements/).
