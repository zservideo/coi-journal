## What prequalification is for

Prequalification answers a narrow question: should this trade be allowed to bid? Turner & Townsend frames it as establishing baseline capability, with project-specific risk evaluated later at buyout. Construction Dive's 2019 survey of GCs described Mortenson sending a qualification package with each request for proposal and independently verifying what came back.

Insurance belongs in that baseline, because a trade that can't buy the coverage your projects require will become a problem after award.

## Before award: can they comply?

The prequalification questions that matter for insurance:

- **Program shape.** Carriers, limits, deductibles and retentions by line. ABC Carolinas' 2026 guidance lists typical ranges for general liability, auto, umbrella and workers comp, plus additional insured status with primary and noncontributory wording and completed operations coverage for three to five years.
- **Willingness.** EPIC lists the trade's willingness to meet insurance requirements as an assessment factor, and reluctance to share safety metrics or claims history as a red flag.
- **Consistency.** EPIC also flags inconsistent or outdated certificates. A trade whose certificates don't match each other is telling you something about its broker relationship.
- **Exclusions against scope.** EPIC calls out coverage exclusions that conflict with the trade's scope, such as height or trade-specific exclusions. Ask for the exclusions schedule now, not after award.
- **Safety record.** Sentry's guidance includes OSHA violations, injury rates, recent losses and experience modification rate. These drive what the trade pays for insurance and whether it can get it.

## After award: prove it for this project

Some proof can't exist until there's a contract. Additional insured endorsements that name your entities, per project aggregate schedules and waiver schedules all depend on the subcontract. Our paper on [reading a COI against requirements](/reports/reading-a-coi-against-requirements/) covers that review.

## Re-qualify on a schedule

Munich Re's 2025 guidance recommends a formal review at engagement and regularly after, with a yearly review as a common practice and more frequent reviews for high-risk phases or new trades. It also recommends rechecking proof of insurance at least yearly or before critical milestones, because policies lapse, cancel or change.

## Red flags worth a call

- Certificates from the same trade that don't agree with each other.
- A broker who won't send endorsements.
- Height, residential or classification exclusions on a trade whose scope runs into them.
- A carrier below your rating floor. See [carrier ratings](/reports/carrier-ratings-in-requirements/).
