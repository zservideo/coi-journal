## The estimate

BCS, which sells COI tracking software, estimates that tracking contractor certificates by hand costs $36,400 a year. The math: 20 hours a week, times 52 weeks, is 1,040 hours, at $35 an hour.

It's the only published figure we found that shows its arithmetic, which is why COI Journal runs it as [The Number](/#num-h).

## How to read it

- **It's a vendor estimate.** BCS has a commercial interest in the number being large. That doesn't make it wrong. It means you should check it against your own company.
- **The 20 hours is the key input.** Our read of the post is that the 20 hours sits at the top of a range BCS says its own clients report saving. It's a marketing estimate, not a time study.
- **It's labor only.** It counts the hours someone spends collecting, logging and chasing certificates.

## What it leaves out

The costs that matter most don't show up in a time sheet.

- **Claims.** A trade's uninsured or underinsured loss that lands on the GC's policy. See [When coverage lapses mid-project](/reports/coverage-lapse-mid-project/).
- **Deductibles and retentions** the GC pays on those claims.
- **Premium.** A worse loss history can mean a costlier renewal.
- **Delay.** Work stopped while a trade fixes its coverage.
- **Owner exposure.** Contract claims from an owner whose requirements weren't met.

None of these can be estimated in general. All of them can be estimated for your company.

## A worksheet for your own number

1. **Volume.** Trades per year, times certificates per trade (initial plus renewals), times endorsements per certificate.
2. **Time.** Minutes to collect, read, compare, chase and file each one. Time yourself on ten.
3. **Rate.** The loaded hourly cost of whoever does it.
4. **Misses.** Over the last five years, how many claims involved a trade whose coverage didn't meet requirements? What did each cost you, including deductible and staff time?
5. **Total.** Labor, plus your average annual cost of misses.
