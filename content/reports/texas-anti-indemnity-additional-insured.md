## The rule

Texas's construction anti-indemnity act, in Chapter 151 of the Insurance Code, voids as against public policy any construction contract provision to the extent it requires indemnity for claims where the indemnitee is at fault, with certain exceptions. Porter Hedges calls the prohibition absolute, compared with the older fair notice doctrine that let carefully drafted clauses survive.

The definition of construction contract is broad. Per Porter Hedges, it includes remodeling, maintenance or repair of improvements to real property, other than a single family house, townhouse, duplex or land development directly related to them.

## The insurance rule

Section 151.104 is the part that touches certificates. It makes a provision in a construction contract that requires additional insured coverage void and unenforceable to the extent it requires or provides coverage the subchapter prohibits for an agreement to indemnify, hold harmless or defend.

In practice: a Texas subcontract can't use an additional insured requirement to get coverage for the GC's own fault that the indemnity clause couldn't get directly.

## The two exceptions

- **Employee claims (151.103).** The prohibition doesn't apply to a provision requiring indemnity for bodily injury or death of an employee of the indemnitor, its agent, or its subcontractor of any tier. Jobsite injuries to the trade's own people are the most common claim against GCs, so this exception carries a lot of weight.
- **Wrap-ups (151.104(b)).** The section doesn't apply to a policy or endorsement issued under a consolidated insurance program to the extent it lists, adds or deletes named insureds. See our brief on [wrap-ups and COIs](/reports/wrap-ups-and-cois/).

## What to do with this

1. **Have counsel review Texas subcontract insurance clauses** against Chapter 151, especially older templates.
2. **Keep collecting additional insured endorsements.** Coverage for the trade's share of fault, completed operations and employee injury claims all still matter. See [additional insured endorsements](/reports/additional-insured-endorsements/).
3. **Know which projects are residential.** The single family, townhouse and duplex carve-out changes the analysis.
4. **Read the endorsement, not the certificate.** Carriers write Texas-specific wordings, and the ACORD 25 won't show them.
