The Number · Data desk

$36,400 a year

One vendor's estimate of the labor cost of tracking contractor COIs by hand, for an organization managing 100 or more active vendors.

20hours a week→× 52weeks→= 1,040hours a year→× $35an hour→= $36,400a year
The markup

Our read: the 20 hours is the top of a 15 to 20 hour range that the vendor says its own clients report saving. It is a marketing estimate, not a time study. We're running it because it's the only published number we found, and we'll replace it when we have our own.

Source: The $36,000 Problem: What Manual COI Tracking Is Really Costing You, BCS (Business Credentialing Services), May 14, 2026.

Business Insurance · Gavin Souter · November 12, 2026

Zurich North American premiums rise 5% as construction business grows

Zurich said its U.S. construction premiums grew 17% in the first nine months of 2026, helped by infrastructure and technology projects. North American property and casualty gross written premiums rose 5% to $18.69 billion while North American rates held flat. Global commercial rates fell 1%, mostly on softer large-property pricing.

Why it mattersBig carriers are growing construction books on flat rates. That's room for brokers to push for the endorsements project requirements ask for, not just price.

Insurance Journal · William Rabb · October 29, 2026

Florida Men Charged With Creating Sham Insurance Now Face Federal Indictment

A federal grand jury indicted two Destin, Florida men tied to The Wave Insurance and Hydrogen Deep, neither licensed to write property and casualty coverage in Florida. Prosecutors allege they collected more than $651,000 in premiums and issued falsified certificates of insurance. Both pleaded not guilty; trial is set for December 14.

Why it mattersThe carrier named on a certificate is worth checking against state insurance records. A COI can name a company that isn't allowed to write the coverage at all.

Business Insurance · Gavin Souter · October 22, 2026

Idaho high court revives injured worker's lawsuit against contractor

In Bauer v. Scott Myers and Sons Roofing, the Idaho Supreme Court revived a plumber's negligence suit against a roofer after a balcony railing the roofer allegedly reinstalled gave way. Whether one trade is shielded from suits by another trade's workers, the court said, turns on whether the GC controls the work, not on contract labels.

Why it mattersTrades on the same job can end up suing each other, which is exactly when real GL limits and correct additional insured endorsements get tested.

The COI Dictionary

The words that decide who pays, with the form numbers

Each entry says what the term means, how it shows up on a certificate, what to check and where it usually breaks.

  • Certificate of insurance (COI)A one-page summary of a contractor's insurance at the moment it was issued.ACORD 25
  • Additional insured (AI)A person or company added to someone else's liability policy, usually the owner and the general contractor on a trade contractor's policy.CG 20 10CG 20 37CG 20 38
  • Completed operationsLiability for injury or damage that shows up after a contractor's work is finished, such as a leak or a collapse months after turnover.CG 20 37
  • Primary and noncontributoryWording that makes the contractor's policy pay first for the additional insured, without asking the additional insured's own policy to share the loss.CG 20 01
  • Waiver of subrogationA promise by the contractor's insurer not to sue you to recover what it paid on a claim.CG 24 04WC 00 03 13
  • Per project aggregateAn endorsement that gives each construction project its own general aggregate limit, so claims on one job can't use up the limit for yours.CG 25 03
  • Notice of cancellationAdvance notice that a contractor's policy is being cancelled.ACORD 25
